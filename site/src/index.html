Lodestone

Reliable AI calls for tools on a shoestring budget, running serverless.

A small library (TypeScript, with an alpha Python port) that sits between your tool and AI models. When a model is withdrawn, slow, rate-limited or down, it moves to the next one, stays inside your time limit, spends only what you allow, and, if everything fails, tells the person what went wrong in plain words.

Who this is forNot platform teams

Made for the person deploying a serverless function on Friday night

Gateways market to platform teams. Lodestone is for indie builders, Global South startups and free public tools: people for whom a fraction of a cent per call matters and a 30 second limit is real.

$0.0004

What it cost to read a job posting on a low-cost paid model, measured on 28 October 2026. At that price the question is not whether you can afford a call, it is whether you can afford a call that fails and is paid for again.

30 s

How long a serverless function has before its gateway cuts it off with an error page nobody can read. Lodestone plans inside 24 s, so one slow model cannot use up the whole time.

Indie builders

One setup, then forget it

A single createLodestone() call replaces the retry, timeout and fallback code you would otherwise write for every tool. It has no dependencies and uses only fetch.

Global South startups

Spend what you chose, no more

Paid-first on your own key with free models as the backup, a daily and a monthly spend limit, and a customer's own key kept apart from yours. Models can also be ordered by measured quality in Hausa, Igbo and Yoruba, through Naija Eval.

Free public tools

Fail in words a person can read

When every model fails, explain() says what happened and what to do, in plain language, and tells a daily limit from a busy service from a missing credit. The person never sees a gateway error.

Not for everyone. If you need streaming, tool calling or embeddings, or you already run a platform team with a gateway, use that. Lodestone does not replace a gateway: services such as Requesty work inside it as fallback steps, so your tool keeps going when a whole gateway has a bad day. See what it does not do yet.

The problem6 ways a call breaks

Six ways a cheap AI call breaks

Free and low-cost models come and go, and gateways have bad days. Every tool that calls them ends up rewriting the same defensive code. Lodestone does it once, for all your tools.

  1. The model disappears

    A hard-coded model id stops working without warning when the provider withdraws it.

    FixYour list is a preference, checked against the provider's live catalogue. Gone or retiring models are dropped and replaced.

  2. One model eats the clock

    A single slow model uses the whole serverless time limit and the person sees an unreadable gateway error.

    FixEach attempt gets a slice of a 24 s budget (under a 30 s gateway). A slow model is cut off and the next one tried.

  3. "429" means four things

    Daily limit, per-minute limit, busy upstream, or no credit. Each needs a different message to the person.

    FixFailures become typed errors, and explain() turns each into advice a person can act on.

  4. The provider itself is down

    One gateway's outage takes every model behind it, and every tool that depends on it.

    FixFallback providers, each with its own key: Requesty, LLM API, Groq and others. An outage cannot use up the whole time budget before they get a turn.

  5. The same dead model, every time

    Free models fail in bursts, and without memory every request pays to find out again.

    FixA circuit breaker, shared across functions, skips a model that keeps failing and lets one attempt through every two minutes to see if it is back.

  6. "Free" is not free if it hangs

    A paid fallback with no limit is risky on a public tool, and free models that never answer cost your users time.

    FixPaid-first on your own key, free as the backup, and a spend hook that stops paid calls at a daily or monthly limit.

What one bad day looked like

On 28 October 2026 the free OpenRouter models behind a real tool stopped answering, and scoring a job posting ran its whole 24 s budget and failed. These are the measurements that changed how Lodestone's defaults work. One day, one posting and one CV, so read them as an example, not a benchmark.

Free models

1 usable answer in 15 attempts

Three attempts each on five free models. Two were rate-limited every time (429), two held the connection open for 45 s and never answered, and the router once chose a safety classifier that replied "User Safety: safe". The one good answer took 25 s.

Paid models

About 3 s, for under a tenth of a cent

Reading a posting: claude-haiku-5.5 took 3.0–3.2 s in all three runs for $0.0004. Scoring took about 5 s. Gemini was 5–6 s; Mistral Small was erratic (7–9 s, and one rate limit).

A mistake worth owning

Time until the whole answer

My first speed table timed only until the response headers arrived, which made every model look much faster. OpenRouter sends headers early and the body follows seconds later. Lodestone's own tests and tools now time the full body.

So reasoning is set per model (openrouter({ reasoning })), a paid-first mode exists (paidFirst), and the free models become the backup. The result for that tool: reading a posting in 2.6–3.1 s and scoring in 4.5–4.9 s, instead of failing after 24 s.
